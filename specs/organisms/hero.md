# Hero

A hero is the first region under the navigation. It names the page.

Guidance follows [What is the Hero Section on a Website?](https://awebco.com/blog/hero-section/).

## Intent

The hero is a container of content, usually above the fold. Above the fold is everything visible before scrolling, including the navigation. The hero is the band under that navigation.

A marketing page uses the hero to say what is offered, why it can be trusted, the benefit, and the next action. This portfolio uses the hero to name the page. The next action stays in the body.

## Default example

Home. Headline "Charlie Koepke". Subheading "UX Designer and Researcher". The background is navy shade blocks.

About uses the same shades as circles and the headline "About Me". Projects uses the shades as lines and the headline "Projects". A case study uses a plain light grey band and the study name.

## Appearance

Home, About, and Projects sit on brand navy `#12263E`. The headline is Montserrat 600 in inverse `#F7FAFC`. On a wide page the headline is 6rem. The home subheading is 32px Gafata, 8px under the name. The pattern is decorative.

The case study hero has no pattern. Its fill is the sunken surface `#E6EDF5`. The title is Montserrat 600 in `#12263E`. Breadcrumbs sit above the title.

The text aligns with the page column. Padding above and below the title is 64px.

## Rules

- The headline is the page `h1`.
- One hero on a page.
- A subheading is one line, and only when it adds the role or the offer. Home is the only page that uses one.
- The pattern is hidden from assistive technology. The headline carries the name.
- A call to action, when the page has one, sits in the body under the hero.
- Do not add a second headline, a video, or a photograph in the hero. The shade pattern, or the plain grey band, is the visual.
- Home, About, and Projects keep their own pattern. A case study uses the plain band.
